Your job is to coordinate strategy and operational priorities, perform final internal approval, review video quality directly, work with AM and team heads, and ensure that evidence follows the work. Holding the Marketing Manager title does not establish unlimited financial, platform or contractual authority. Confirm those limits with the agency owner.

### The five separate approval/readiness milestones

| Milestone | What it enables | What remains blocked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Client ready to sign AFTER meeting one | AM compares initial proposal with actual brief and develops actual strategy with SM/MM/MB. | Signature, folder movement and contracted production are not implied. |
| Contract and four-part pack signed | Move same client folder from main Quotations to standalone client-named folder. | Production remains blocked until required upfront funds are verified. |
| Required upfront payment verified | Contracted delivery within agreed scope. | Head/MM/client asset approvals are still required later. |
| Quality review + MM final internal approval | AM can gather finalized work into one client PPTX. | Publication is blocked pending final client approval of that version. |
| Final client approval | Approved calendar/presentation handed to SM; approved Design/Video files archived. | Changed content is not automatically covered by old approval; paid activation remains separate. |

### Three things that look like progress but are not authorization

• A first meeting, a positive reaction or a client asking for a quotation is not automatically readiness to sign.

• A moved folder or a signed contract is not proof that the upfront payment was verified.

• “Done”, “Final approval”, a daily-finished entry or “edits done” is not automatically external client approval.

### The first question whenever you are unsure

Ask: Which milestone is actually evidenced, who made that decision, which work/version does it cover, and what is the next permitted action? If the answer is unclear, record the gap and use the agreed authority rather than inventing a bypass.

## 4B. Shoot logistics, source-media collection and equipment custody

CONFIRMED split: AM arranges outsourced videography/photography, podcast guests and PR person. SM collects data from the videographer/photographer, hands it to editors/designers, takes full accountability for memory cards and studio tools, and shoots behind-the-scenes content. Outsourced crew, guests and PR person are external stakeholders; this does not add new internal positions to the 14-role map.

### Before the shoot — suggested control checklist

1. AM confirms the agreed brief, outsourced participants, guest/PR arrangements, schedule/location and responsible contacts. Exact forms, response deadlines and commercial booking authority require confirmation.

2. AM and SM agree the required footage/photos, intended recipient editor/designer and BTS scope so collection matches the task.

3. SM identifies memory cards and studio tools to be used and records their issue/current custody in the adopted inventory. Asset IDs and issue/return forms are recommended controls.

4. If a company hard drive is needed, record which one, its location and its named custodian; do not assume it is a verified backup.

5. Clarify permissions for guest/client/BTS material through the agreed scope and authorized people. This guide does not invent consent or usage rights.

### After the shoot — collection and handoff

1. SM collects the data from the videographer/photographer and keeps the task/brief context with it.

2. Identify originals, relevant task/account and intended holder; recommended folders/labels should be agreed before renaming existing files.

3. Hand the relevant files/references to the assigned editor/designer. A WhatsApp message saying “sent” is not evidence that files are usable.

4. Recommended acceptance check: receiver confirms the required files are present and open, and reports missing/corrupt material to SM/AM. This is a proposed verification control.

5. Record BTS output/reference and distinguish internal source media from any externally approved publication. BTS capture does not authorize publishing unapproved material.

6. SM accounts for memory cards/studio tools and reports missing items or damage. Temporary physical possession by a crew member does not erase SM’s confirmed accountability.

7. Where media is stored on a company hard drive, keep the drive/path and custodian in the adopted transfer record. Card/tool responsibility does not automatically settle hard-drive ownership.

### Do not format or overwrite as a workaround — recommended safeguard

A recommended company policy is to prohibit formatting/clearing a card or overwriting originals until transfer, readability, required backup and authorization are confirmed by the adopted process. The user did not specify a retention period, number of backups or format authority, so those must be approved rather than invented. If media is unreadable or missing, log the issue and seek authorized recovery; do not experiment destructively.

## 5. Your daily operating loop — proposed routine

No fixed times, meeting durations, SLAs or escalation thresholds were confirmed. Use the following as a proposed sequence, adjusted to workload and agreed responsibilities. The purpose is to reveal the next action and owner, not to create unnecessary meetings.

### Opening control check with AM and relevant heads

1. Open the current task sheet and approved calendar view, not a saved screenshot.

2. Check promised client dates and today’s review/publication dependencies.

3. List blockers: missing brief/footage/copy, unresolved comments, pending head/MM/client decisions and access/payment evidence.

4. Confirm one accountable holder and next action for each priority item.

5. Check that work waiting for you is genuinely review-ready; return incomplete submissions with specific missing inputs.

6. Resolve competing priorities through the agreed authority; record changed expectations and AM follow-up.

### During execution

• Let holders execute and heads manage their quality layer; avoid an invisible parallel set of assignments.

• Use review windows/queues appropriate to workload, without presenting unagreed timing as a company SLA.

• Make decisions against the actual brief and output version. If a strategy conflict appears, involve AM and the relevant SM/MB contributors.

• Record feedback once in a traceable place and connect it to the task/calendar.

• Ask AM to surface client requests and scope changes before the team commits to extra work.

### Before a client presentation or release

1. Check head/video quality decisions and MM final internal approvals for included work.

2. Check AM’s one-PPTX manifest and version.

3. Check the client’s final decision applies to that version; missing approval holds publication.

4. Check final Design/Video copies and SM’s calendar/asset links match approved work.

5. Keep paid activation authorizations and media funding separate from organic content approval.

### Closing control check

1. Reconcile the task sheet with holder updates and the daily-finished log.

2. Carry unresolved comments forward visibly; do not close tasks to make a daily report look better.

3. Record waiting states separately: waiting for holder, head, MM, client, inputs, payment/access or scope decision.

4. Name the next action owner and next check point for every carryover.

5. Brief AM on what can move to client review and what remains blocked.

### What a useful daily summary contains

Account/project; current stage; tasks completed with evidence; approvals pending by actor; unresolved comments; blockers; next action/holder; client decision needed; changed commitments. Do not fabricate turnaround, utilization or overdue numbers without reliable dates and definitions.

### A proposed prioritization lens

First protect approval and financial gates. Then consider client commitments, dependency impact, readiness, effort/capacity and business importance. ### When two reviewers disagree

Ask what each is trying to achieve against the brief. Identify the actual conflict and affected item. With AM and relevant decision-makers, settle one executable direction; record the final instruction and which prior comment is superseded. Do not make a holder guess which authority to follow.

## 8. Exception playbook and decision trees

### A positive first meeting but no readiness confirmation

Decision question: Do we have explicit readiness to sign?

Route: No: Sales/BD and AM keep a next pre-sales action. The actual-strategy group does not start.

Avoid: Do not allocate the actual-strategy work simply because the meeting felt successful.

### Ready to sign, but real brief conflicts with the proposal

Decision question: Is actual-strategy scope aligned?

Route: AM compares the proposal and brief with SM/MM/MB; resolve gaps and align quotation before signing pack.

Avoid: Do not promise unchanged price/scope if the strategy materially changes the workload.

### Signed client folder exists but payment is not verified

Decision question: Have signed pack AND required upfront funds been confirmed?

Route: Hold contracted production; obtain verification from the authorized financial owner.

Avoid: A moved folder, receipt screenshot or demo check is not proof of verified funds.

### Missing footage or incomplete brief

Decision question: Can the holder meet acceptance requirements with the inputs?

Route: Mark blocked and identify the missing input; AM coordinates the supplying party and revised next action.

Avoid: Do not pass incomplete work downstream or blame a holder for unavailable inputs.

### Head/MM/AM/client requests edits

Decision question: Are all comments executed and re-reviewed?

Route: Keep/reopen task; identify holder; record executable comments and owner responses; repeat required approvals.

Avoid: Do not equate “edits done” with approval or close unresolved comments.

### Different reviewers give incompatible directions

Decision question: Can the holder execute both without contradiction?

Route: AM/MM/relevant head settle one direction tied to the brief; record what supersedes the earlier instruction.

Avoid: Do not ask the holder to choose an authority privately.

Avoid: Do not create a self-approval or financial override.

### Wrong asset is already live

Route: Record the issue, stop further unreviewed release, involve AM/MM/authorized platform operator and follow the agreed correction route.

Avoid: This manual does not create a blanket delete/refund/public-statement authority.

### Performance data or actual-spend evidence is missing

Decision question: Can the claim or fee be verified?

Route: Mark unverified, request source access/evidence and do not invent a result or final fee.

Avoid: Do not use planned budget as actual expense.

### Suspected extra scope or client complaint

Decision question: Is the request inside the signed scope and agreed authority?

Route: AM coordinates clarification with the responsible commercial/leadership decision-maker and records next action.

Avoid: Do not silently commit extra work, credits or terms outside delegated authority.

## 9A. Monthly performance appraisals: how to run the people review

CONFIRMED: conduct the company performance appraisal monthly. This is distinct from client performance reports, daily task checks and the quarterly objectives section inside the forms. Use the actual month and approved role-specific master, not an assumed annual or quarterly review cycle.

| Section in supplied forms | Weight |

| Company events and culture | 10% |

| Score | Level | Behavioral description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Poor | Performance consistently falls below expectations |
| 2 | Below Expectations | Occasionally meets expectations; needs improvement |
| 3 | Meets Expectations | Consistently meets job requirements |
| 4 | Exceeds Expectations | Frequently exceeds job requirements |
| 5 | Outstanding | Consistently exceptional performance |

### Prepare, meet and follow through

1. Select the approved role-specific form and record employee, actual review month and confirmed direct reviewer privately. Do not infer a reviewer from coordination responsibilities.

2. Collect month-specific evidence from tasks, quality/revision records, outputs and role-appropriate results. Distinguish missing inputs or changed scope from individual execution.

3. Employee completes Self-Rating before the review meeting. Manager completes Manager Rating; compare the gap rather than assuming agreement.

4. Use the supplied 1–5 scale and behavioral anchors. Ratings 1, 2 and 5 require a brief evidence-based justification in the Notes field/section.

5. Check the role-specific weights and calculations. Supplied Excel uses Gap = Manager minus Self and weighted contribution = KPI weight × Manager Rating / 5. Confirm completion before interpreting totals: blank manager ratings are treated as zero in the supplied formulas.

6. Hold the monthly review conversation, record achievements, blockers, strengths, improvement areas and employee feedback. Track the template's quarterly OKRs within the monthly conversation; do not confuse the two cadences.

7. Agree SMART goals and development/training actions for the next period, with accountable action owners and agreed follow-up. Record the signatures/decisions required by the adopted process.

8. Keep completed appraisals and salary/career information in private authorized storage; carry goals into the following monthly review. Promotion, salary, succession and HR fields are not automatic decisions or approvals.

### Read the formula correctly

The sampled Excel formulas calculate Gap = Manager Rating minus Self-Rating. Each weighted contribution is KPI weight × Manager Rating / 5, and section contributions are summed. The employee self-rating supports discussion; it is not substituted for manager rating in those formulas. Some blank manager ratings produce zero contributions, so completeness must be checked before interpreting a total or grade.

Sampled grade formulas show Excellent at 90%+, Very Good at 80%+, Good at 70%+, otherwise Needs Improvement. Treat these as provided-template logic, verify the approved master and all required ratings before use, and do not turn an incomplete form into a negative employment judgment.
